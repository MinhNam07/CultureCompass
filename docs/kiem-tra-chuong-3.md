### [ ] C-VM-KHUEVAN
> Khuê Văn Các was built in 1805 under the Nguyễn: two storeys and eight roofs, brick pillars below and wood above, with round windows whose spokes stand for the rays of the Khuê star. Scholars’ best poems were read there, and it has been taken as the symbol of Hà Nội.

### [ ] C-ALTAR
> Many homes of the Việt majority have an ancestral altar with photographs of family members who have died, and offerings of incense, flowers, fruit and water. Ancestors are honoured most on a death anniversary (giỗ), on the 1st and 15th of the lunar month and above all at Tết.

### [ ] C-SWORD-LEGEND
> In the legend, after the victory over the Ming in 1428, Lê Lợi was boating on the lake when a golden turtle rose and asked for his magic sword; he returned it, and the lake, once called Lục Thủy (“Green Water”), became Hoàn Kiếm, the Lake of the Returned Sword. ### [ ] C-NGOCSON
> Ngọc Sơn temple stands on an islet in the lake; documents kept there date it to 1841, and it was restored in 1865 by the scholar Nguyễn Văn Siêu. ## B. Đoạn biên tập (editorial, không có claim) — đọc lại giọng văn

- [ ] **Vietnam’s first university**: Calling it a “university” is a modern shorthand: it trained scholars for the royal examinations in the Confucian classics, not in the subjects a university teaches today.
- [ ] **The family altar**: Our sources say nothing about guests and altars. Leaving the altar and its offerings alone, and asking before taking a photo of it, are the safe choices.
- [ ] **Chùa, đền, đình: which is which?**: Trấn Quốc on West Lake is a chùa. Ngọc Sơn on Hoàn Kiếm Lake is a đền. Văn Miếu, the Temple of Literature, is something else again: a temple to Confucius.
- [ ] **Tết, the Lunar New Year**: If you are in Hà Nội at Tết and not invited anywhere, it is kinder not to drop in on a family on the first day.
- [ ] **The Mother Goddesses**: Phủ Tây Hồ, on a spit of land in West Lake, is one Hà Nội shrine to Mother Liễu Hạnh. A ritual you come across is worship, not a show: watch quietly and ask before filming.
