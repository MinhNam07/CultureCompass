About CultureCompass

A student UX prototype that helps travellers prepare for a few everyday situations in Hanoi through short interactive scenes.

How the content was made

  • Each scene is limited to one setting — for example a family homestay, or one named heritage site — and says so.
  • Every point links to its source. Official and site-specific sources were used first; secondary sources are marked Being verified.
  • Site rules (like those of the Temple of Literature) are kept separate from general customs and CultureCompass tips.
  • Where a source is silent — for example on photography at the Temple of Literature — the scene says so and suggests asking.
  • Right now 0 points and phrases are still being verified.

What the feedback means

  • ✓ Fits this situation — a good choice here, with the reason.
  • ⇄ Another way that also fits — there is often more than one good way.
  • ? Needs more context — worth rethinking; here’s why.
  • i See the rules and guidance — a law, a place’s written rule or official guidance says what to do.

    Accessibility features

    • Every object in a picture also has a labelled button; everything works with a keyboard.
    • Reduce motion and text-first settings show actions as still steps with the same information.
    • Every phrase has its written form and meaning; audio never plays automatically.
    • Feedback uses words and icons, not just colour. There are no timers.

    These choices follow W3C guidance; this prototype has not been audited for WCAG conformance.

    Prototype scope

    One destination (Hanoi), four scenes. No accounts, bookings, AI or tracking. Not included: legal, visa or medical advice — please use official sources for those. The street scene quotes the pedestrian rules of Vietnam’s road traffic law so you know they exist; it is not legal advice.
